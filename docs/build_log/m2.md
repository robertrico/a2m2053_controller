Disk in throughout, tab set to protected. Supply limits +5V 1.0 A, +12V 1.5 A.

CA0 fall 5.5 ns, RD rise 13.9 ns. The M1 screen estimate was 36 ns.

## Step 1: motor and tach

`motor on` -> `OK` (READY low within 2 s). `status 4` MOTORON=0, `status 13`
READY=0. `tach` at track 0: 196 edges in 0.5 s = **392 rpm** (ERS zone 1:
394 rpm, -0.5 %).

Scope cross-check: `status 7` puts /TACH on RD. Scope `measure`: **392.93 Hz**,
48.9 % duty. At 60 pulses per revolution that is 392.9 rpm, so the firmware's
60-pulse assumption holds. `docs/captures/m2/2026-10-10_tach_track0_2ms.png`.

Supply with the motor running: +12V 0.13 A, +5V 0.14 A.

### +5V drop at the connector

| Supply setpoint | Motor | CN104 pin 11 (meter) |
|---|---|---|
| 5.00 V | off | 4.90 V |
| 5.00 V | on | 4.78 V |
| 5.15 V | on | 4.84 V |

Raising the setpoint 0.15 V gained only 0.06 V at the connector, so the drop
is not a fixed resistance. Likely a poor contact (banana post, breadboard
rail, ribbon tap) in the +5V or GND path. The supply stays at 5.15 V for now.
Fix the wiring before M3: a direct +5V/GND run to the CN104 tap, then check
supply terminals vs breadboard rail vs pin 11 with the motor on.

Watching pin 11 on the scope (CH3, 100 mV/div, DC) did not work: the scope
read 0.16 V below the meter and motor switching spikes of +-0.3 V triggered
it. The BW-limit setting did not stick and force-trigger was ignored.

## Step 3: step timing

| Measurement | Time |
|---|---|
| Full-stroke seek (79 tracks) incl. console, 3 runs | 0.975 / 0.981 / 0.982 s |
| Console round trip alone (`status 0`) | 0.49 s |
| Seek itself | about 0.49 s = about 6.2 ms per track |

Much faster than the ERS 36 ms single-step figure. /STEP goes high as soon
as a step is counted (ERS 3.2.4.2), so the firmware issues steps quickly and
the mechanism's destination counter moves the head behind them. The time
includes the READY wait. READY came back within that time even across the
394 -> 590 rpm zone change, and `tach` right after read 588 rpm.

## Step 4: eject (fails: mechanical)

| Attempt | Firmware | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `eject` | ROM-style short strobe (2 us), 3 tries | `ERR eject failed` after 5.2 s. No sound, disk stays in. |
| `eject` | LSTRB held 500 ms (`087297d`, from tashnotes mci.md) | `ERR eject failed` after 6.7 s. No sound. |
| `cmd 14` + `status 6` | short strobe, raw | EJECT flag (address 6) goes high, clears about 2 s later; CSTIN stays 0 |
| `cmd 14`, watching the supply | short strobe, raw | +12V rises from 0 to about 0.28 A for the 2 s window; no sound or movement |

ERS 699-0452: EJECT (address 6) goes high on the LSTRB rising edge and low
when CSTIN rises or after 2 s max (3.2.4.4). Eject strobe width is 1 us min,
300 ms max (3.4.7), so the ROM's short strobe is in spec and the 500 ms hold
was not; reverted in `9f9eb3f`. Eject motor current is 500 mA on +12V (2.4).

Conclusion: the drive accepts the command and powers the eject motor, which
stalls. Disk insertion is also sticky on this drive. Most likely hardened
grease in the eject gear train. Rico is cleaning the mechanism and checking
whether this is the drive he repaired before.

Note: address 6 is EJECT in the unified-drive ERS (699-0452). The firmware
names it SWITCHED (tashnotes naming).
