## Capture path works first time

First capture: `FLUX BEGIN 0` with a valid CRC, 2.14 s round trip (2 s window
plus dump), so the firmware path and USB dump were fine from the start. The
histogram of the first good capture (Drive A, T0 S0) shows three peaks at
2.0, 4.0 and 6.0 us, which matches the 2.0-count PIO overhead fix.
`probe` confirmed the default byte order GROUP_ORDER = CHECKSUM_ORDER = (0, 1, 2).

## The read failures were the +5V supply path

For most of the session reads failed: 0 intervals, or patchy captures that
dropped out mid-capture and noise-only histograms (0.8-2 us smear).

What it looked like:
- RD on the read-data address (8 / 9) sat at about 0.4 V with ~0.23 V p-p
  noise, at the CN104 pin and at the mechanism's own RD test point.
- Drive A gave patchy data after a head-flex reseat; Drive B gave nothing.
- Status reads, tach, seek, recal and eject all worked throughout.

What it was: resistance in the +5V/GND path between the bench supply and the
drive. With the motor running, CN104 pin 11 measured as low as 4.01 V
(pin 13: 11.57 V); ERS minimum is 4.75 V. The supply stayed in CV, current
well under 0.5 A, so it was IR drop in contacts and jumpers, not a load fault.
Switching either channel moved the other (shared return resistance). After
reworking the contacts, and later adding bulk caps at the tap, both drives
read every sector.

Ruled out along the way: firmware, decoder, disk (readback byte-identical),
write mode (tab protected, no erase), head position, heads lifted.

## Design requirement for the PCB

The drive's +5V and +12V and GND need low-resistance paths: wide traces or
pours from the input connector to CN104, all four CN104 GND pins tied, and
bulk capacitance at CN104 on both rails, as the original IF-83 board has:
4.7 uF 50 V electrolytic (read off the IF-83). Use the same part.
The read amplifier fails silently when +5V sags at the connector.
